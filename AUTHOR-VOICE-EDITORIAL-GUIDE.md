# Author voice and attribution — editorial guide

The author names in article front matter should remain attached to the drafts only with the relevant contributors’ permission. This pass improves repetitive phrasing but does not claim that an author personally typed every sentence.

## Voice by contributor role

- **Jon Pill — game strategy and casino games:** lead with the mechanic or decision the reader needs to understand; use compact explanations, clear distinctions, and concrete checks. Avoid presenting strategy as a way to beat randomness.
- **Dan Michalski — gambling, casinos, media and technology:** prioritise evidence, systems, incentives, and risk; distinguish data from marketing claims and explain uncertainty plainly.
- **Corey Levitan — gambling and entertainment journalism:** use readable, lively framing around the game’s theme and player-facing experience, then anchor each claim in the rules. Do not invent first-person play sessions or personal anecdotes.
- **Marco Ibarra — iGaming writing and editing:** structure complex topics for quick scanning, define terms on first use, and remove redundant explanations.
- **Dominic Field — casino, slots, poker and sports betting:** use practical comparisons and explain where a rule or feature changes the reader’s understanding; avoid unsupported recommendations.
- **Cristina Tudose — iGaming content and editorial SEO:** use precise headings and natural search language without keyword stuffing; keep answers direct and non-repetitive.
- **Arthur Mustoe — casino and slot reviews:** prioritise game rules, interface details, paytable checks, and clear distinctions between verified specifications and impressions.

## Non-negotiable editorial rules

1. Do not invent quotes, first-hand testing, credentials, or personal opinions for a named contributor.
2. Keep RTP, limits, bonus terms, licensing, and availability qualified by version, operator, and jurisdiction where relevant.
3. Add source links for material factual claims and verify the live version before publication.
4. Author attribution does not itself establish that the named person reviewed or approved this exact draft. Record approval only after it is obtained for the final text.
5. Translate for natural local usage; do not translate idioms literally or use word count by spaces as a quality metric for Japanese.
